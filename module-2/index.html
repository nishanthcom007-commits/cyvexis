// 01. UNDERSTAND THE MECHANICS

How Attackers Weaponize Stolen Credentials

Modern identity attacks do not rely on "movie-style" brute force. They rely on high-volume automation and psychological exploitation. Understanding their playbook strips away their advantage.

ATT&CK T1110.004 High Volume
🌊

Credential Stuffing

When a third-party website suffers a breach, millions of email/password combinations are leaked onto dark-web forums. Attackers use automated botnets to "stuff" those exact credentials across thousands of unrelated services (work email, cloud consoles, banking, and SaaS).

The Attacker's Leverage: Password re-use. If you use the same password on an obscure hobby forum as you do on your primary account, one breach unlocks both.
ATT&CK T1110.003 Stealth Technique
🎯

Password Spraying

Instead of trying 1,000 passwords against 1 account (which triggers instant account lockouts), attackers try 1 common password (e.g., Summer2024! or Company123#) against thousands of known corporate usernames in stealth mode.

The Attacker's Leverage: Predictable company naming standards and predictable seasonal passwords that evade traditional lockout thresholds.
ATT&CK T1621 Psychological Warfare
💣

MFA Fatigue (Prompt Bombing)

The attacker already bought or scraped your password, but hits an MFA prompt. They deliberately trigger dozens of push notifications in rapid succession—often in the middle of the night—accompanied by spoofed IT messages or spam calls, aiming to wear down your cognitive resistance.

The Psychological Mechanism: Sensory overload, fatigue, and desperation to stop the annoying phone buzzing. The victim approves just to silence the device.
🤝

Our Core Philosophy: Zero User-Shaming

Falling victim to prompt bombing is NOT an indicator of user error or negligence. MFA fatigue is an aggressive, intentional form of psychological harassment engineered to exploit human cognitive limits and sleep disruption. Security is a collaborative partnership: our job is to equip you with calm reflexes, safe tools, and immediate non-punitive support.

// 03. DECIDE WITH CONFIDENCE

The Modern Credential Defense Hierarchy

Passwords alone are obsolete. Defending against modern identity attacks requires establishing layered barriers that make automated credential abuse economically unviable for attackers.

🗄️
FOUNDATIONAL

Password Managers

Eliminates human memory vulnerability and defeats credential stuffing at its root.

  • Zero Re-use: Generates unique 20+ character random credentials for every single site.
  • Built-in Anti-Phishing: Authenticators match the exact cryptographic domain. If you are on a counterfeit site, the manager refuses to autofill.
  • Recommended Tools: 1Password, Bitwarden, Apple Keychain, or corporate-managed vaults.
🧱
HIGH ENTROPY

Passphrases > Complex Strings

Length trumps artificial complexity every single time against modern GPU cracking rigs.

  • Length Is King: A 4-word passphrase like correct-horse-battery-staple has vastly more cryptographic entropy than an 8-character P@$$w0rd!.
  • Memorability: Easy to type on mobile, harder for automated rainbow tables to crack.
  • Golden Rule: At least 4-5 unrelated words, minimum 16+ total characters.
🛡️
DEFENSE RANKING

MFA Methods Compared

Not all Multi-Factor Authentication is created equal. Know your protection level:

🥇 S-TIER: PASSKEYS & FIDO2 HARDWARE

YubiKeys and WebAuthn / Passkeys. Completely phishing-resistant and immune to prompt bombing because authorization is bound to the physical hardware chip.

🥈 A-TIER: NUMBER MATCHING PUSH

Microsoft/Google Authenticator with Number Matching enabled. Requires typing a 2-digit number shown on the login screen into the phone, totally defeating blind fatigue taps.

🥉 D-TIER: SMS / VOICE CODES

Vulnerable to SIM-swapping, SS7 telecom interception, and real-time reverse-proxy phishing (Evilginx). Use only if zero other MFA options exist.

// 04. RESPOND & CONTAIN

Accidentally Tapped "Approve"? Here Is Your Containment Protocol

Panic freezes action; a clear checklist restores control. If you tapped "Approve" under fatigue or duress, follow these 3 emergency steps immediately.

01

Change Your Password Immediately

Immediate • Minute 0-2

The attacker has your password—that is how they triggered the MFA prompt in the first place. Immediately change your master password or account password from a clean, secure session.

Crucial Tip: If possible, change the password from a separate clean device (like your phone) if you suspect your primary laptop could be compromised.
02

Terminate All Active Sessions & Revoke Tokens

Immediate • Minute 2-5

Changing your password might not automatically kick an attacker out if they have an active OAuth session token or cookie. Navigate to your security settings (e.g., Google Security, Microsoft MySignIns) and click:

🔴 "Sign out of all web sessions / Revoke all active devices"
03

Report to Your IT Admin / Security Operations Team

Minute 5-10

Do not hide it out of fear of getting into trouble. Good security teams celebrate early reporting because it transforms a minor event into a successful defense.

What SOC Teams Do When You Report: They immediately block the attacker's IP subnet, check tenant logs for unauthorized file downloads, and audit conditional access policies.
💚

Remember the Golden Rule of Security Operations:

"We would rather investigate 100 accidental approvals reported in 5 minutes than 1 breach discovered 6 months later by auditors."

// 06. KEY TAKEAWAYS

Your Daily Credential Defense Checklist

Check off these habits to harden your personal and organizational identity against credential attacks.

1. Unprompted Push = Compromised Password

If you receive an MFA push when you weren't actively logging in, your password is already in the hands of an attacker. Deny the prompt and reset your password immediately.

2. Never Approve to Silence the Noise

Notification fatigue is an intentional attack tactic. Approving the request hands the keys to your company's network to a criminal. Treat repeated prompts as an ongoing incident.

3. Eliminate Password Re-use with a Vault

A breach on one service should never threaten another. Use a password manager to store unique, complex 20+ character passwords for every account.

4. Upgrade to Phishing-Resistant MFA

Prefer Passkeys, FIDO2 hardware keys, or Number Matching over simple "one-tap" approvals and SMS codes whenever available in your account settings.

5. Rapid Reporting Trumps Embarrassment

Security is a team sport. If you ever accidentally tap approve, immediately report it to your IT/SOC team. Quick reporting neutralizes the breach before data leaves the network.
